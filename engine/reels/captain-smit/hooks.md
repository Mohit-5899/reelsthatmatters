# Hooks: Captain Smit Machchhar and flydubai FZ1073

## 1. The myth broken
"A locked cockpit door keeps a plane safe. Unless the danger is inside."
Picture: a thick reinforced cockpit door, bolts shut, drawn in cross-section; a red outline of the threat appears on the cockpit side of it, not the cabin side.
Why it works: it states the sourced myth and turns it in the same breath, and the rest of the reel (the door opening) answers it. (Myth; facts 9, 25)

## 2. A question a child would ask
"What happens when one of the two pilots is the threat?"
Picture: a simple top-down cockpit with two seats; one seat glows warm, the other turns red, the locked door behind them.
Why it works: an honest question with no easy answer, and it names the real problem without naming the co-pilot. (Facts 9, 18)

## 3. A number that sounds wrong
"Flydubai 1073 fell more than 15,000 feet in under two minutes."
Picture: an altitude trace, flat at 34,000 ft, then a near-vertical line dropping to the 17,000 to 19,000 ft band.
Why it works: one hard, drawable number that shows something went badly wrong, before the reel says what. (Fact 7)

## 4. The person
"Badly wounded, Captain Smit Machchhar still had more than 170 people to protect."
Picture: a 737 MAX 8 cruising, seen from the side, with rows of small seat dots filling the cabin behind a single cockpit window.
Why it works: it puts the captain and the stakes first, so the reel is about what he did, not about the attacker. (Facts 3, 10, 16; head count)

## 5. The twist in who landed it
"Flydubai 1073 was landed by two pilots who were only riding along."
Picture: two figures seated in the cabin among passengers, then a dotted line drawing them forward to the cockpit; the map line bends to Tabuk.
Why it works: a surprising true detail that pulls the viewer back to how the plane got there. (Facts 13, 14)
