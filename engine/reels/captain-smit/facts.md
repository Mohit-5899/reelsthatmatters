# Captain Smit Machchhar and flydubai FZ1073 (30 September 2026)

## The one-line answer
When his co-pilot attacked him inside the locked cockpit of flydubai FZ1073, the wounded captain, Smit Machchhar, got the door open so people from the cabin could stop the attacker. Two flydubai pilots who were on board for the return flight then landed the jet safely in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia.

## The myth
"A locked cockpit door keeps a plane safe." Reinforced doors are built to keep attackers *out*. They do nothing when the threat is already inside, and here the attacker was one of the two pilots behind the door [1][14]. In this case, opening the door saved the plane. (The same weakness was seen in Germanwings 9525 in 2015, when a co-pilot locked the captain out [1].)

## Facts
1. Flight FZ1073, Dubai (DXB) to Tel Aviv (TLV), 30 September 2026, diverted after what flydubai first called an "incident" and then "an altercation" on the flight deck [2][3][4] (High: airline statement plus outlets).
2. The aircraft was a Boeing 737 MAX 8, registration A6-FKF [3][16]. ToI also says 737 MAX [4] (High).
3. The captain, Smit Machchhar, is Indian, from Mumbai (Al Jazeera: "a suburb of Mumbai"), and lives in Dubai with his wife and two children [5][6][12] (High).
4. His career: about 15 years in aviation, about 9,750 flying hours, more than 11 years at SpiceJet, and with flydubai since 2022 [6][12] (High: two outlets agree, both citing his LinkedIn).
5. He was the pilot flying, and he was 38 [16] (Medium: Wikipedia citing WSJ and News18; we did not read those pages).
6. The attack came about 2 h 15 min into the flight, in Saudi airspace near the Jordan border [4][16] (High; Al Jazeera says "nearly two and a half hours" [6]).
7. The jet dropped from about 34,000 ft to about 17,000 ft in roughly a minute [4][6] (High; AvHerald gives FL340 to about 19,000 ft in about 90 s [3]).
8. The transponder squawked 7700 (emergency) at 05:28 GMT and then 7500 (unlawful interference) at 05:35 GMT [1] (Medium; AvHerald gives the codes in the reverse order [3]).
9. The UAE Attorney-General said the co-pilot attacked the captain with the cockpit's crash/emergency axe and tried to seize the controls. A forensic report found head injuries consistent with that weapon [7][8] (High: official statement).
10. Badly wounded, the captain released the cockpit door from inside so that people could get in. Israeli PM spokesman Doron Spielman said he used "the last moments of his energy" [5][6][11] (High: Israeli officials and several outlets).
11. The UAE Attorney-General's account (9 Oct) adds to this. A flydubai pilot assigned to the return flight noticed the cockpit door "opening and closing repeatedly", entered with the access code, and with one cabin crew member and several passengers rescued the captain and restrained the attacker [8][9] (High: official).
12. Several Israeli passengers helped overpower the attacker [4][5] (High).
13. The return-flight captain and his co-pilot, who were on board but not operating this flight, took control and landed at Tabuk [8][9][3]. flydubai's own words were "on-duty flydubai crew travelling on the flight" [2] (High: official plus airline).
14. They landed at Tabuk (TUU) about an hour later, or about 85 minutes after leaving cruise altitude [3][6][4] (High).
15. flydubai said all passengers were safe and accounted for, and ToI reported no passengers seriously injured [2][4] (High).
16. The captain was seriously injured and treated in Tabuk. He was then moved to Abu Dhabi, and on about 2 to 3 Oct the Indian Embassy said he was "recovering well" [6][7][3] (High). flydubai's CEO said he returned to the UAE after medical clearance [2]. We found no later update on his condition.
17. The co-pilot was injured, held by Saudi authorities, and moved to the UAE on 1 Oct [6][7] (High). His name is left out on purpose.
18. On 3 Oct the UAE Attorney-General said the co-pilot "had attempted to carry out a terrorist act" [7] (High: official).
19. On 9 Oct UAE prosecutors said he had planned to seize the plane and crash it into the Ben Gurion Airport passenger terminal as a suicide attack. He confessed with his lawyer present and says he acted alone, and investigators are checking for accomplices [8][10] (High: official). **Motive: not established.** Prosecutors said he described his motives but did not publish them [8]. ToI reports the plan as "inspired by" 9/11 [10] (Medium).
20. The investigations are led by the UAE, with Israel joining. AvHerald also reports a probe by Saudi Arabia's NTSC [3][7] (High).
21. Part of the rudder was missing after landing, probably torn off during the high-speed dive [4]. AvHerald reports "substantial damage" [3] (Medium).
22. Netanyahu called him "a true hero" who saved 174 people. Modi called him a hero and said India is proud [6][12] (High).
23. flydubai suspended its Tel Aviv flights while the investigation runs [2] (High).
24. On 27 Oct 2026 the UAE President conferred the Order of Zayed II (First Class) on the FZ1073 crew, including the captain. Sheikh Hamdan presented the medals [11][16] (Medium: one outlet read, and Wikipedia agrees).
25. US rules require the flight-deck door to resist forcible intrusion and a 250 lb pull on its handholds, and to stop handgun rounds (NIJ IIIa). Its lock can be unlocked only from inside the flight deck [14][15] (High: regulation).
26. On a 737 the door normally stays locked in flight (AUTO mode). Crew can request entry, an emergency keypad code opens it after a delay unless a pilot cancels, and ICAO requires it to be lockable and unlockable from either pilot seat [1] (Medium: one good outlet).
27. Indian outlets compared him to Neerja Bhanot, the Pan Am 73 purser killed during the September 1986 Karachi hijacking (379 on board) while shielding children, who was posthumously awarded the Ashoka Chakra. Gulf News framed it as "40 years apart" [13] (Medium).

## Disagreements between sources
- **Head count:** flydubai CEO says 172 passengers *and crew*; AvHerald says 174 + 6 = 180; Netanyahu and Gulf News say 174; Wikipedia says 172 + 8 = 180. We found no source for a "147" figure.
- **Crew count:** AvHerald says 6, Wikipedia says 8.
- **Door:** Israeli officials say the captain released it from inside. The UAE AG says the return-flight pilot entered with the access code after seeing the door open and close repeatedly. Both can be true, but neither confirms the other.
- **Who flew:** "off-duty pilots" (Israel and passengers), "on-duty crew travelling" (flydubai), "return-flight captain and his co-pilot" (UAE AG). ToI quotes Israel's defence minister as saying "another flight crew present on board".
- **Timing:** about 2 h 15 min into the flight (ToI) vs "nearly 2.5 h" with an 8:44 local distress call (Al Jazeera) vs 7700 at 05:28 GMT (Al Jazeera feature).
- **Dive:** to 17,000 ft in under a minute vs to 19,000 ft in 90 s. Squawk order also differs.
- **Weapon:** passengers described a pocket knife (per AvHerald); the UAE AG says an axe.
