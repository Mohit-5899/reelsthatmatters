A locked cockpit door keeps a plane safe. Unless the danger is inside.

Cockpit doors are built to keep attackers out. Under American rules, a door must hold against being forced, and stop handgun bullets. Its lock opens only from inside.

On the thirtieth of September, flydubai flight ten seventy-three left Dubai for Tel Aviv. More than two hours in, over Saudi Arabia near Jordan, the co-pilot attacked the captain inside the locked cockpit. The jet dived, falling more than fifteen thousand feet in under two minutes.

The captain was Smit Machchhar, from Mumbai. He was seriously injured. But the cockpit door came open, and help got in. Crew and passengers held the attacker down.

Two flydubai pilots on board for the return flight took the controls. About an hour later, they landed safely at Tabuk, in Saudi Arabia. flydubai said every passenger was safe.

The captain was treated in Tabuk, and the Indian Embassy said he was recovering well. India's Prime Minister called him a hero.

A locked door is meant to keep danger out. On this flight, the door that opened is what saved the plane.
