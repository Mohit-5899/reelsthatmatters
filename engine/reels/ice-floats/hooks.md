# Hooks: Why ice floats

## 1. The myth broken
"Cold water sinks. So why does frozen water float on top?"
Picture: Real 3D glass of water, side view. An ice cube drops in, starts to sink as the viewer expects, then stops and bobs back up to the surface.
Why it works: It uses the intuition everyone has (colder means heavier, true for water down to 4 °C, fact 9) and breaks it in the first two seconds.

## 2. A number that sounds wrong
"Freeze one litre of water and it grows to one point oh nine."
Picture: 2D diagram of a sealed cube marked "1.000 L" at a fill line; it frosts over and the ice rises past the line to "1.09 L".
Why it works: Things are supposed to shrink when they freeze; a precise number that goes the wrong way makes people stay to hear why (facts 1-2).

## 3. The iceberg
"About ninety percent of an iceberg hides underwater. So why float at all?"
Picture: Real 3D iceberg cut away at the waterline, the small white tip above, the huge blue mass below, a "~90%" label on the hidden part.
Why it works: A famous image with a twist: if ice is that close to sinking, what keeps it up is the question the reel answers (fact 12).

## 4. What it saves
"Ice floats, so ponds don't freeze solid. But why does ice float?"
Picture: Real 3D lake cross-section in winter: an ice cap on top, dark water below, a fish swimming near the bottom.
Why it works: It gives the stakes first (fish survive winter, fact 10) and then turns to the cause, so the physics feels like it matters.

## 5. Zoom in
"Ice floats because frozen water molecules lock into a roomy hexagonal cage."
Picture: Real 3D close-up of jostling liquid water molecules that slow, snap into place and pull back to reveal the open hexagonal ice lattice.
Why it works: It gives the answer at once and promises the thing only this reel can show: the cage itself, moving (facts 4-5, one-line answer).
